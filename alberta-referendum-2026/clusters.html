Ridings that vote alike

Same lean, different priorities

Most of the differences between Alberta's ridings come down to one thing: how strongly they voted Yes overall. A riding that leaned Yes on immigration also leaned Yes on the Senate, on judges and on the rest of the ballot. That overall leaning accounts for 99% of the differences between ridings. Of the 87 ridings, 27 voted Yes on all of the first nine questions and 24 voted No on all nine. The most Yes-leaning ridings are mostly rural. The 12 least Yes-leaning are all in Edmonton and Calgary.

This page sets that leaning aside. For each riding, it asks which questions drew more or less Yes than the riding's overall leaning would predict. These differences are small, usually a few percentage points. Grouping ridings by that pattern gives two clusters. Each one mixes city, suburban and rural ridings.

The two clusters are mirror images. They differ most on the separation question, set against the immigration and social-program questions. In the first, ridings such as Calgary-Bhullar-McCall and Edmonton-Meadows voted less for a referendum than their other answers suggest, and slightly more Yes on the immigration and social-program questions. In the second, ridings such as Peace River, Lesser Slave Lake, Cardston-Siksika and Edmonton-Strathcona voted more for a referendum, and slightly less Yes on those other questions. Edmonton-City Centre is in the second cluster mainly because it voted less Yes on the immigration and program questions and more on the constitutional ones. No single question stands out the way the overall leaning does.

This analysis uses CBC News's final results, saved on October 28, 2026, with 5,536 of 5,537 polls reported. The clusters are built from the 54 ridings where every question was fully counted and the pattern was large enough to tell apart from counting noise. The other 33 are placed provisionally and hatched on the map. Results are unofficial until Elections Alberta certifies them, and riding results describe places, not how any individual voted.

How we did this

The results come from CBC News's live results feed for all 87 ridings. The maps use Elections Alberta's 2019 riding boundaries. The grouping uses final results saved on October 28, 2026, when 5,536 of 5,537 polls had reported.

For each riding, we took the share of Yes votes on each question, counting Referendum as Yes on Question 10. We split these into two measures. A riding's overall leaning is how strongly it voted Yes across the whole ballot. Its issue profile is a list of ten numbers, one per question, showing how much more or less Yes it voted on that question than its leaning predicts. We set the overall leaning aside, removing the part of each question that moves in step with it, and grouped ridings by issue profile alone. To compare two ridings, we used cosine similarity, which measures how closely their two lists point in the same direction. It is 1 when two ridings depart from their leaning on the same questions in the same proportions, 0 when their patterns are unrelated, and −1 when they are opposite. It ignores how large the departures are, so a riding with strong departures can match one with mild departures of the same shape. A standard clustering method, k-means, then sorted ridings with similar patterns into groups.

To check whether the groups were stable, we reran the grouping 200 times on reshuffled samples of ridings. The groups held up well, with a stability score of 0.98 out of 1. Two kinds of ridings were left out of the grouping and placed afterward. In some, a question had less than 80% of its usual vote count, because later questions are often counted after the first. Many others departed so little from their overall leaning that their pattern could not be told apart from counting noise. Both kinds are hatched on the map.

To explain the groups, we trained a model to reproduce them. We then measured how much each question mattered to its decisions, using a technique called SHAP, and repeated this check 30 times. All of these patterns describe ridings, not how any individual voted.
