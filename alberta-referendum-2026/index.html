Alberta referendum 2026 – results by riding

What Alberta said

Alberta is staying put. CBC News projects that a majority of Albertans voted to remain in Canada. About three in four voters chose Remain on Question 10, rejecting a move to start the legal process toward a binding separation referendum.

The other nine questions, on immigration, social programs, voter ID, judges, the Senate and federal powers, were far closer. Most sit within ten points of an even split, and a couple are close enough that the rest of the count could still tip them. The one clear winner is requiring proof of citizenship to vote, which nearly two in three voters supported.

Where people live told you more than which question they were answering. Rural central Alberta leaned Yes on almost everything; the cores of Edmonton and Calgary leaned No on almost everything. The ridings that vote alike page maps those patterns, and the handful of questions where ridings broke from them.

This summary is based on partial results: 4,887 of 5,583 polls reported, as observed at about 2:30 p.m. MDT on October 20, 2026. The figures in the list below and on the maps update from CBC News's live feed every hour. All results are unofficial until Elections Alberta certifies them.

    How we did this

    The results come from CBC News's live results feed for all 87 ridings, using Elections Alberta's 2019 riding boundaries. For the grouping on the ridings that vote alike page, we used results as observed at 1:52 p.m. MDT on October 20, 2026, when 4,841 of 5,583 polls had reported. For each riding, we took the share of Yes votes on each of the 10 questions (Referendum on Question 10) and split it into two parts: the riding's overall leaning, meaning how strongly it voted Yes across the whole ballot, and its issue profile, meaning the questions where it voted more or less Yes than that leaning would predict. A standard clustering method (k-means) then sorted ridings with similar numbers into groups. To test whether the groups were real, we reran the grouping 200 times on reshuffled samples of ridings. The overall-leaning groups held up well in these repeated tests (a stability score of 0.85 out of 1); the issue-profile groups less so (0.61), which is why we call them exploratory. Ridings with fewer than half their polls counted were left out of the grouping and placed afterward; they are hatched on the grouping map. To explain the groups, we trained a model to reproduce them and measured how much each question mattered to its decisions (a technique called SHAP), repeating the check 30 times. These are riding-level patterns. They describe places, not how individuals voted.
